Koett pill: three concepts

Every pill below is drawn at its real logical size (1 CSS px = 1 point on macOS, 1 GTK logical px on Linux) on a dark and a light desktop. Today's pill is 236 × 48 and shows a rolling waveform and a timer. Each concept covers six states: idle/hover, recording, drawing (Shift held), transcribing, done and error.

What the best pills do

  • Wispr Flow: a small bar sits at the bottom of the screen at rest. You click it to dictate, right-click it to snooze it, and you can drag it to either edge. While recording it shows white level bars, and Esc cancels. It never shows live text: everything pastes at once when you release.
  • Superwhisper: you can switch between a mini window (waveform and status only) and a large one that shows the mode. Right-click opens Expand, Settings and History. An optional always-on indicator starts recording or switches mode on hover. Users complain that it keeps reverting to the large view.
  • Scrawl: press ⌥⇧S, talk, and drag with Shift held to draw. Shift-click drops a numbered pin, ⌘Z undoes and Esc cancels. Each spoken sentence is paired with the screen frame it was about. Numbered pins work well, and they're the model for Koett's sketch count.
  • macOS Dictation: a small mic popover near the caret, with streaming text written straight into the field. Live text builds confidence, but it needs a streaming model.
  • Granola: a quiet floating level meter during meetings and nothing else. It shows how far restraint can go.

Useful mid-dictation, or clutter

  • Useful: proof that it's hearing you (real level), elapsed time, what you've drawn and where it sits in your speech, a cancel that works without the mouse, and something to do when paste fails.
  • Useful only with streaming: live words. Phrase-by-phrase text that arrives a beat late is still reassuring. Text that flickers or rewrites itself is worse than none.
  • Clutter: a language chip (Parakeet v3 auto-detects, but Koett doesn't receive the detected language), word counters while recording, model names in the main view, and decorative motion with no input behind it.
  • Rule kept from today: the pill never takes focus, and silence never raises a warning.

Recommendation: C, Timeline

The pill becomes a record of this dictation, not just a level meter. Your whole recording sits in one strip, silence shows as dots (Silero already finds it), and each sketch is a numbered coral band at the moment you drew it. That's the one thing no other dictation pill shows, and it makes the Shift-to-draw feature legible: you can see that sketch 2 belongs to "this button here".

  • No new model needed. Every model can drive it today. The transcribing sweep uses real progress: Parakeet already decodes VAD segments one by one, so segments done out of total is an honest fraction.
  • Stays a pill. At 300 × 48 it's only 64 px wider than today. Actions appear on hover or after the dictation ends, so the default view stays calm.
  • Grows into B later. When Nemotron, or a phrase-commit Parakeet path, is ready, the "last phrase" line from B can go in C's hover row without redesigning anything.
  • Why not A: it's the safe polish, but it barely answers "there has to be more on the pill". Why not B: it only works as designed on the English-only streaming model. With Parakeet it shows text one phrase late and burns CPU decoding during capture.

Feasibility notes

  • Live partial text: true streaming text exists only with Nemotron 560 ms (CrossPlatform/src/transcription/nemotron.rs, English only, labelled "Test" in the Mac menu). Parakeet v2/v3 are offline models. The approximation is to decode each Silero segment as it closes (after a ≥0.5 s pause), which puts text a phrase behind and runs ASR during capture. The final pass stays authoritative.
  • Clicks during recording: today the pill has an empty input region and keyboard mode "none", and the docs say recording stays pointer-transparent. Hover actions need an input region on the pill itself, still without focus. That works on layer-shell and on a non-activating NSPanel. GNOME and X11 have no layer-shell, so they keep in-window feedback.
  • Esc to cancel: needs a temporary global key while recording: the GlobalShortcuts portal on Wayland, a grab on X11, and an event tap on Mac. Shift is already taken by the draw worker, so the pill only reflects that state and never captures it.
  • Sketch data: the draw worker must emit start/end timestamps and an index per sketch. Bands and pins are drawn from those, never inferred.

Motion, all concepts

  • Pill width changes run at 220 ms on cubic-bezier(0.23,1,0.32,1), and content cross-fades with a 2 px blur. Appearing and disappearing are instant: they follow a keypress you make many times a day.
  • The drawing ring appears at 120 ms, and the waveform keeps moving because audio is still being recorded.
  • Bars and the strip follow measured RMS at 30 fps, and nothing moves without input. With reduced motion, bars are static, the strip adds a column per second, and the timer, counts and states still update.
  • Done stays up for 10 s (the current value), or until the pointer leaves after hovering. Errors stay until you act on them or for 30 s.
